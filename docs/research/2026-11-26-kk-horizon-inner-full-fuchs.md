# 2026-11-26 — Local uniqueness for all gauge-fixed linear vacuum components on the inner KK collar

## Result and scope

Follow-up to #288. Work on the exact fixed analytic background of #270, g=-2 dr dv+f dv²+r² gamma, f=1-s²/r², gamma the unit hyperbolic metric, v periodic with period L. On the chronology-violating side s-d<r<s, consider either a covector (5 components) or a symmetric covariant two-tensor (15 components) U solving a homogeneous linear wave equation (Box_g+V)U=0, where V is any analytic, v-independent bundle endomorphism regular through r=s. In particular this includes the gauge-fixed linear vacuum Einstein equation with curvature endomorphism Vh=2R_acbd h^cd, and the homogeneous residual gauge-vector wave equation. Assume U smooth in regular coordinates up to r=s and vanishing to order at least three there (U, partial_r U, partial_r² U all zero). Then U vanishes throughout the connected product collar. This is a local theorem without global spatial integrability or assumed analyticity of U. If the first three horizon jets are v-independent, apply it to partial_v U to obtain circle invariance. The background coefficients are analytic and v-independent. For the metric interpretation the harmonic/de Donder gauge condition is assumed, not proved attainable for arbitrary smooth inner vacuum perturbations.

## 1. Regular coordinate wave operator and diagonal indicial weights

Retain the regular coordinate components of the bundle. Write C_mu for the covariant connection matrix, so nabla_mu=partial_mu+C_mu in these components. The wave operator has scalar principal part, the scalar wave drift, a matrix first-order term A^mu=2g^(mu nu)C_nu, and a regular analytic zeroth-order matrix. Direct Christoffel evaluation at the horizon gives, on covector components ordered (r,v,x,y,z),
    C_v(s)=diag(1,-1,0,0,0)/s.
On a symmetric covariant tensor the weights add, since the connection acts on both slots. Thus W=s C_v(s) is a constant diagonal matrix with weights -2,-1,0,1,2 and multiplicities 1,3,7,3,1 for rank two, and weights -1,0,1 with multiplicities 1,3,1 for rank one. The script checks these matrices in the actual EF coordinate coframe. In particular, no singular frame change is used.

Let u=s-r>0, F=f(s-u)=-u(2s-u)/(s-u)^2, D=u partial_u. Fourier analyze in the periodic v variable, with fixed real k=2pi n/L and complex component vector T(u,x). Multiplying the wave equation by -u²/F gives an equation whose scalar radial part is
    D²+(B-1)D+C,  B=u(-f'-3f/r-2ik)/F, C=3ik u²/(r F).
The screen second-order term has coefficient H=-u²/(r²F)=u/(2s-u) times gamma^AB. The additional matrix radial drift is (u/F)A^r D, with A^r=-2 f C_r-2 C_v. Hence (u/F)A^r tends to W and B tends to 1+iks. All other matrix terms, including the v derivative replaced by ik and the potential, are analytic O(u). The difference of the radial coefficient from its indicial value is also analytic O(u). Consequently the equation is
    [D²+(iks I+W)D+R] T=0,
where R consists of analytic O(u) coefficients multiplying D, spatial derivatives through order two, or the identity. There are no mixed D/spatial derivatives in R; the spatial second-order coefficient is scalar times the component identity. This last fact is essential for the scalarization below. The actual connection and radial limits have been checked for both bundle ranks. The background, its inverse, connection and curvature are analytic in a collar away from z=0 and r=0; the O(u) claims for the potential follow from the explicit multiplication factor.

## 2. Common monic scalar indicial polynomial

By the vanishing assumption write T=u³ S with S smooth up to u=0 (Taylor's integral remainder). Conjugating by u³ replaces D by D+3. For a component of weight w, the leading operator is
    p_w(D)=(D+3)(D+3+iks+w).
All roots have strictly negative real part, including at k=0. Define
    P(D)=(D+3) product_{w=-2}^{2} (D+3+iks+w).
This is monic of degree six. For each component i of weight w_i, multiply its row equation on the left by the constant coefficient Euler polynomial
    a_i(D)=product_{w=-2,...,2; w!=w_i} (D+3+iks+w).
It is monic of degree four and a_i p_{w_i}=P, including coinciding roots at k=0. This construction works for rank one using the same polynomial, even though two weights are absent. Applying the row operators to the equations, not dividing by a differential operator, gives
    P(D) S + sum M_{j,alpha}(u,x) partial_x^alpha D^j S=0.
All coefficient matrices M are analytic O(u): differentiation by D preserves that order. The terms satisfy j+|alpha|<=6, with j<=5. The only possibly total-order-six term with |alpha|=2 and j=4 has a scalar coefficient times the identity: the leading fourth power of every row multiplier is the same, and the original second-order screen term was scalar. All other terms with |alpha|=2 have j<=3. For |alpha|=1, j<=4; for |alpha|=0, j<=5. This bookkeeping is audited symbolically, including derivatives landing on coefficients.

## 3. Exact scalar lift of the resulting system

Introduce N auxiliary real variables b_i (N=5 or 15) and the linear polynomial p=sum b_i S_i. Encode a coefficient matrix M acting on S by the scalar operator sum b_i M_ij partial_(b_j), which is exactly correct on these polynomials. Do not use this additional derivative for a scalar coefficient times the identity; use multiplication instead. For the leading common P(D), use the scalar polynomial directly. The order audit in Section 2 ensures that the scalar lifted equation has order at most six with no extra term D^6:
    [P(D)+sum c_{j,alpha,beta}(u,x,b) partial_x^alpha partial_b^beta D^j] p=0,
where j<=5, j+|alpha|+|beta|<=6 and |alpha|+|beta|<=3. Every coefficient of the sum is O(u), analytic in u,x and polynomial in b. In particular the lifted indicial polynomial is exactly P, not a fiber-variable differential operator. A naive direct scalar lift of the original system would leave unweighted fiber derivatives in the indicial operator; the common row polynomial eliminates that obstruction. The auxiliary variables are not physical fields or extra spacetime dimensions.

Apply Lope, *Existence and Uniqueness Theorems for a Class of Linear Fuchsian Partial Differential Equations*, J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 6 (1999), 527–538, Theorem 2 and conditions (A-1)–(A-3), (NE), pp.528–529, https://www.ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp/journal/pdf/jms060304.pdf . The operator is monic in D of order six, the lower Euler/tangential derivative orders meet the theorem's total-order restriction, and choosing weight mu(u)=u^(1/3) makes all tangential coefficient quotients by mu^d continuous at zero (d<=3, coefficients O(u)). The integral of mu/u is finite. On a sufficiently small complex tangential neighborhood all coefficients are bounded holomorphic in the tangential variables and continuous in u. All roots of P have real parts <=-1. Smoothness of S gives continuous Euler derivatives through order six in the distribution space, using a tangential cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of the chosen point. No equation is needed outside the smaller local product region. The theorem therefore gives p=0 near u=0 and that point. Since p is a linear polynomial, each component S_i is zero there.

For each fixed k the original vector equation is an elliptic system on the interior u>0: the scalar second-order symbol has positive radial coefficient -F and positive transverse metric divided by r². Its coefficients are analytic. The analytic interior regularity theorem for elliptic systems (Morrey–Nirenberg, CPAM 10 (1957), 271–290) and the identity theorem propagate zero throughout the connected collar. This avoids any need to choose a single uniform Fuchsian neighborhood for all Fourier modes. Fourier completeness now gives U=0. If the first three horizon jets of U are invariant under v translation, apply the result to partial_v U since the background operator commutes with that derivative.

## 4. What is and is not established about gauge and nonlinear rigidity

For a smooth linear vacuum metric perturbation h satisfying D_g(h)=nabla^a h_ab-1/2 nabla_b tr_g h=0, the homogeneous wave equation of Section 1 follows by the usual linearized Ricci identity. Thus all fifteen components are covered, including circle and mixed components, not just TT screen components. The homogeneous covector gauge equation is also covered. However, on the inner side we have not solved the *inhomogeneous* wave equation for a flat gauge correction for a general smooth flat source. The exterior Cauchy-limit construction of #285 cannot simply be imported because r is spacelike here. Hence we do not claim arbitrary-gauge uniqueness modulo gauge from this result. For example a flat vector field exp(-1/u²) sin(kv) partial_v produces a smooth flat nonzero pure-gauge metric perturbation for k!=0. Linearized Ricci vanishes by diffeomorphism invariance. This illustrates the need for a gauge condition; it is not a counterexample to gauge-fixed uniqueness. Neither the general smooth v-dependent nonlinear coefficients of #286 nor two-sided nonlinear rigidity have been addressed. Petersen's compact-horizon theorem (https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09135) remains relevant prior work with different hypotheses. No general priority claim.
